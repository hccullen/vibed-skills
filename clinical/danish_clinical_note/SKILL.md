# Danish Clinical Note — Language, Style, and Semantics

Write Danish clinical notes that read as if a Danish-trained healthcare professional wrote them for a colleague who will take over the patient's care. The journal is a **kommunikationsmiddel** — a communication tool between professionals — and the language must serve that function above all else.

The governing standard is **normen for almindelig anerkendt faglig standard**: the norm for generally accepted professional practice, enforced by Styrelsen for Patientklager (STPK). Language that falls below this norm — vague, ambiguous, or uninterpretable to a colleague — is criticisable even when the clinical decision was sound.

See [REFERENCE.md](REFERENCE.md) for the full legal framework, prescription structure, drug form abbreviations, medicinordination requirements, and STPK enforcement patterns.

## PSOAP — the Danish journal disposition

Danish clinical practice uses **PSOAP**, a five-part disposition that structures the note so a colleague can scan it and act:

- **P — Problem:** The patient's presenting complaint as stated, not your interpretation. For an unvisited patient: the symptom (*svie ved vandladning*, not *UVI*). For a chronic-disease follow-up: the diagnosis (*KOL*, *diabetes*).
- **S — Sygehistorie:** The patient's subjective symptoms — present ones primarily, but absence of key symptoms can also be noted (*hovedpine siden i morges, ingen feber eller kvalme*). The five F's (frekvens, forværring, funktion, forløb, følelse) where relevant.
- **O — Observation:** What you found on objective and paraaklinisk examination. Focused and sufficient — not just *Ryg;ia*, but which examinations within the back examination were performed. Vital signs, examination findings, test results.
- **A — Analyse:** Your clinical reasoning — tentative diagnosis, treatment, prognosis, and handling. Overvejelser about the further plan for investigation and treatment. Time can be diagnostically and therapeutically significant.
- **P — Plan:** What happens next: (i) further investigation, (ii) treatment, (iii) arrangements — new consultation/phone contact, (iv) safety netting — when to return.

Write each section so a colleague taking over can pick up the patient without asking you a question.

## Register — the voice of the journal

Write in a **factual, professional register** that a colleague can scan and act on. The journal is not a narrative; it is a working tool. Every sentence should convey information a colleague needs — not what the writer thought, felt, or intended, but what was observed, decided, and done.

**Danish is the language of the journal.** Write the note in Danish. Use Latin and Greek anatomical and diagnostic terms where they are the established fagudtryk — *femur*, *erythema*, *dyspnoe*, *status epilepticus* — because these are the terms a Danish-trained colleague expects. Do not translate established medical terms into Danish prose (*knoglelængde* for *femur*) unless the Danish term is itself the standard (*hjerte* is standard, not *cor*).

The distinction is **alment kendte fagudtryk**: terms broadly recognised across the Danish healthcare system. A term known only within a narrow specialty is not alment kendt — spell it out or use the Danish equivalent for colleagues outside that specialty.

The journal must be **entydig og struktureret** — unambiguous and structured — so information can be found quickly. Journalføringen skal være *entydig, systematisk, forståelig og overskuelig*.

## Abbreviations — the comprehension test

The legal test (§9 stk.5 / §4 in the vejledning) is whether the abbreviation is **forståelig for andre autoriserede sundhedspersoner og disses medhjælp** — understandable to other authorised professionals and their assistants who participate in the patient's treatment. *Der må anvendes fagligt anerkendte forkortelser.*

- **Acceptable:** internationally standardised abbreviations widely used in Danish clinical practice — *BP*, *ECG*, *MR*, *CT*, *CRP*, *INR*
- **Acceptable:** Danish clinical shorthand any Danish healthcare professional would recognise — *indl.*, *udskr.*, *amb.*, *kons.*, *recidiv*, *status praesens*
- **Acceptable:** traditional pharmaceutical abbreviations on prescriptions — *Rp.*, *No.*, *d.s.*, *tabl.*, *mixt.*, *inj.*, *supp.*, *caps.*, *amp.* (see [REFERENCE.md](REFERENCE.md) for the full list)
- **Not acceptable:** specialty-internal abbreviations, personal shorthand, or non-standard letter-and-slash combinations that a colleague from another specialty cannot decode

When in doubt, spell it out. An abbreviation that forces a colleague to stop and decode has failed its function as a communication tool.

## Standardtekster og makroer — template language

*Standardtekster, -fraser m.v. kan i egnet omfang anvendes i journalen. Det skal dog altid sikres, at standardtekster eller -fraser er meningsfulde og tilpasset den konkrete patientbehandling, og at der kun fremgår oplysninger, som er relevante.*

Every template insertion must be checked against what actually happened. If the template says *"Patienten har fået mundtlig og skriftlig information om indgrebet, herunder risici for komplikationer, og har givet informeret samtykke"* — this must be literally true for this patient at this contact. If the information given differed from what the template describes, the template must be edited to reflect what was actually said.

## Objectivity — observation vs interpretation

Separate what was **observed** from what was **inferred**. The journal records both, but the language must make the distinction visible to the reader.

**Observed** — state as fact, in the present or past tense:
- *"Palpabel resistens i højre øvre kvadrant"*
- *"Patienten er vågen og orienteret"*
- *"Blodtryk 145/90, puls 78, temp 37.2"*

**Inferred** — mark as clinical assessment, not as established fact:
- *"Mistænkt cholecystitis"* — not *"cholecystitis"* unless confirmed
- *"Sandsynligvis funktionelle gener"* — not *"funktionelle gener"*
- *"Vurderes at have nedsat compliance"* — not *"nedsat compliance"*

The word **"vurderes"** is the key hedge — it marks the statement as clinical judgement, not established fact. Use it whenever the claim rests on interpretation rather than direct observation.

In the **O** section of PSOAP, state what you found, not the process of examining. Not *"Jeg palperede abdomen og fandt..."* — write *"Abdomen blød, ikke palpable tumorer, ingen rebound."*

## Hedging and uncertainty — calibrate the epistemic level

Danish clinical writing uses a calibrated vocabulary for certainty. Match the word to the evidence:

| Danish | Meaning | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| *påvist* | demonstrated | confirmed by investigation |
| *verificeret* | verified | confirmed by a definitive test |
| *mistænkt* | suspected | clinical suspicion, not yet confirmed |
| *sandsynligvis* | probably | likely but not confirmed |
| *muligvis* | possibly | cannot be excluded |
| *ikke udelukket* | not excluded | differential still open |
| *ikke påvist* | not demonstrated | investigation done, finding absent |
| *afventer* | pending | awaiting results |

Do not over-hedge: if the diagnosis is confirmed by blood test, write *"påvist"* — not *"sandsynligvis"*. Do not under-hedge: if it is a clinical suspicion, write *"mistænkt"* — not *"påvist"*.

## Relevans og nødvendighed — what to write and what to cut

The **relevans- og nødvendighedskriteriet** governs every sentence: include only what is relevant and necessary for the patient's treatment. This is a linguistic discipline, not just a content filter — it means writing with **economy**.

From the vejledning: *"Oplysninger er nødvendige, når de understøtter, at de involverede sundhedspersoner kan udføre undersøgelse, behandling, opfølgning m.v."*

- Write what a colleague taking over the patient needs to know
- Do not write what every entry in this patient's record already says, unless it has changed
- Do not write what the patient's test results already state in a linked report — refer to them
- Normal findings shall be journalført when they have significance for diagnosis, continued investigation, and correct treatment

Over-documentation is not compliance. A note that buries critical information in boilerplate has failed the relevans- og nødvendighedskriteriet even though it contains everything.

## Phrasing patterns — PSOAP in practice

**P — Problem:** State what brought the patient in, in one phrase. *"Henvist pga. progredierende dyspnoe i 3 uger."* Not a retelling of the full history. Avoid interpreting: *svie ved vandladning*, not *UVI*.

**S — Sygehistorie:** Symptoms in the patient's words, plus absence of key symptoms. *"Hovedpine siden i morges, ingen feber eller kvalme."* The five F's where relevant.

**O — Observation:** Findings, not process. *"Abdomen blød, ikke palpable tumorer, ingen rebound. Lever ikke palpabel."* *"Ryg: inspektion ua., palpation: ingen spændinger, SLR negativt bilateralt."*

**A — Analyse:** Connect findings to conclusion. *"Dyspnoe og hævede ben taler for hjerteinsufficiens. Differential: lungeemboli ikke udelukket."* Include overvejelser about further investigation and treatment. Tid can be diagnostically significant.

**P — Plan:** State what will happen, who is responsible, and the timeframe. *"Starter furosemid 40 mg x1. Kontroller natrium og kreatinin om 3 dage. Ny konsultation hos egen læge om 1 uge."* Include safety-netting: *"Ved forværring af dyspnoe eller brystsmerter: kontakt akutmodtagelsen."*

## Receptskrivning — the language of prescriptions

A recept is structured by tradition and law. The notation is:

```
#
Rp. [lægemiddelform] [handelsnavn] [styrke]
No.: [mængde]
d.s.: [brugsanvisning/dosering og indikation]
```

- **# (invocatio):** The double-cross opening. Traditional, marks the start of the prescription.
- **Rp. (recipe):** "Tag" — the instruction to the pharmacist. The most common Danish abbreviation for recipe. R., Rec. also occur.
- **Ordinatio:** The drug. For farmaceutiske specialiteter: the registered brand name (e.g. *Furix®*), not the generic name alone. If multiple specialities share the name, add the firma name (*Furosemid 1A Farma*). Lægemiddelform in abbreviation before the name (*tabl. Furix 70 mg*) or in Danish after (*Furix tabletter*). Styrke must be stated if multiple strengths exist.
- **No. (numero):** The quantity to dispense — e.g. *100 stk.*
- **d.s. (detur signatura):** "Udlever med påskriften" — the brugsanvisning. Must be in **letforståeligt dansk** because it is printed on the medicinpakke the patient receives. Must contain: dosering (enkeltdosis and døgndosis), indikation, and administration instructions where relevant (*indåndes*, *påsmøres*, *indtages med måltid*).

**Ej S** (ej substitution): Write when the patient must receive exactly the prescribed preparation — the pharmacy cannot substitute to the cheapest equivalent.

**Tilskud:** Write *"Tilskud"* under d.s. when the patient qualifies for klausuleret tilskud.

**Off-label:** When prescribing outside the approved produktresumé, journalfør indikation, begrundelse for off-label behandlingen, and the informerede samtykke.

See [REFERENCE.md](REFERENCE.md) for the full prescription structure, drug form abbreviations, dispensing groups, and medicinordination journalføring requirements.

## Medicinordination in the journal

When journalføring a medicinordination, the journal must contain:

- Navn på lægemidlet
- Styrke
- Mængde
- Dosering
- Administrationsmåde
- Hyppighed or the intervaller at which it is given
- Eventuelle doseringstidspunkter
- Behandlingsvarighed / when treatment should stop
- Hvem der har ordineret lægemidlet, og hvordan

The ordination must be in an **enstrenget system** — a single-threaded system so there cannot be modstridende oplysninger about a given ordination. On hospitals this is the electronic medicinordinationssystem, not the prose journal.

## Tone — professional, not personal

Write in the **third person** about the patient. The journal is not a personal log — *"Patienten"* not *"min patient"*.

Do not use evaluative or emotional language about the patient. *"Ikke-kompliant"* is a clinical assessment when supported; *"svær patient"* is a personal judgement with no clinical content. Describe the behaviour, not the person: *"Patienten afviser at tage ordineret medicin"* not *"patienten er ikke-kompliant"* unless the pattern is established and documented.

Avoid stigmatising language. A patient who uses substances is not *"stofmisbruger"* as an identity — describe the use: *"Patienten har et forbrug af heroin, senest i går."*

## Information and consent — the language of samtykke

*Der er ingen formkrav til, hvordan et informeret samtykke skal fremgå af journalen, men oplysningerne i journalen skal tilpasses den enkelte patient og omstændighederne.*

Record what was said and whether consent was given. Not *"Patienten har fået information og givet samtykke"* — that is a template. *"Patienten har fået mundtlig information om fordele og risici ved behandling med metformin, herunder risiko for laktacidose. Giver informeret samtykke til behandling."*

*Kravet til journalføringen af informationen øges, jo mere kompleks behandlingen er, og jo større risiko der er for udvikling af komplikationer.* Omvendt, if the patient knows the treatment and there are no changes to prior information, it suffices to journal that the patient is informed and consents.

If the patient **fravælger** offered examination or treatment, journalfør this, and the information given about consequences (*helbredsmæssige eller økonomiske konsekvenser*).

If the patient has **frabedt sig information** (retten til ikke at vide), this must be noted.

**Stiltiende samtykke:** For almindelige deleelementer in an examination or treatment course where the patient's agreement is udtryklige from the situation (e.g., the patient extends their arm for blood pressure measurement), the samtykke does not need to be journalført directly — but the journalnotat must show it was a treatment situation covered by stiltiende samtykke. If in doubt, indhent udtrykkeligt samtykke and journalfør it.

## Corrections — the language of amendment

*Oplysninger i journalen må ikke slettes eller gøres ulæselige.* Corrections are **additive**, not destructive:

*"Tilføjelse d. 15/3: Ovenstående notat angår venstre, ikke højre knæ. Korrektion foretaget af [navn, titel]."*

In an electronic journal, the original version must remain accessible after any edit. The correction is a new note, not an overwrite. *Slåfejl og skrivefejl må rettes i forbindelse med selve journalføringen* — but after the note is complete, only additive corrections.

## Kritiske fund — VAF, VUF, VIF

When communicating critical or unexpected findings by telephone or in person, the documentation must capture both **what was said** and **that it was received**. Danish clinical practice uses a tiered terminology:

| Term | Meaning | Urgency |
|---|---|---|
| **VAF** | Vigtigt Abnormt Fund — life-threatening or serious, immediate notification | Within the minute |
| **VUF** | Vigtigt Uventet Fund — surprising finding not related to the presenting problem, needs further investigation | Within hours |
| **VIF** | Vigtigt Ikke-akut Fund — important but not urgent | Normal procedure, expedited |

**Closed-loop communication pattern** — journalfør:

1. **Entydig identifikation** of the patient (navn + personnummer, closed loop) and accessionnummer if relevant
2. **Modtageren noterer** tidspunkt, patientidentifikation, and undersøgelsesresultat
3. **Read-back verification** — modtageren læser tidspunkt, identifikation og resultat op for svarafgiveren
4. **Gensidig identifikation** — modtageren noterer svarafgivers navn/brugerkode; svarafgiveren noterer modtagerens navn/brugerkode
5. **Sikring af videreformidling** — modtageren sikrer at svaret bringes videre til den rette fagperson

The key linguistic principle: write not only *what* was found but *what was said, to whom, and when*. *"Fund konsulteret telefonisk med [navn, afdeling] kl. [tidspunkt]. Patienten er informeret. [Modtagernavn] har modtaget besked og bekræftet forståelse."*
