# NHS UK Clinical Record-Keeping — Legal & Regulatory Framework

The exact legal and regulatory citations underpinning the SKILL.md. **LAW** (statute/regulation, enforceable) is distinguished from **GUIDANCE** (GMC/NHS code of practice/professional standard).

---

## 1. GMC — Good Medical Practice (2024)

- **URL:** https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/the-professional-standards/good-medical-practice
- **PDF:** https://www.gmc-uk.org/cdn/documents/good-medical-practice-2024---english_pdf-102607294.pdf
- **Type:** GUIDANCE (professional standards — binding on all GMC registrants)
- **In effect from:** 30 January 2024 (replaces the 2013 edition)

### Domain 3 — "Recording your work clearly, accurately, and legibly" (paras 69–71)

**Paragraph 69:**
> "You must make sure that formal records of your work (including patients' records) are clear, accurate, contemporaneous⁶ and legible."

Footnote 6: "Contemporaneous means making records at the same time as the events you are recording, or as soon as possible afterwards."

**Paragraph 70:**
> "You should take a proportionate approach to the level of detail but patients' records should usually include:
> a. relevant clinical findings
> b. drugs, investigations or treatments proposed, provided or prescribed
> c. the information shared with patients
> d. concerns or preferences expressed by the patient that might be relevant to their ongoing care, and whether these were addressed
> e. information about any reasonable adjustments and communication support preferences
> f. decisions made, actions agreed (including decisions to take no action) and when/whether decisions should be reviewed
> g. who is creating the record and when."

**Paragraph 71:**
> "You must keep records that contain personal information about patients, colleagues or others securely, and in line with any data protection law requirements and you must follow our guidance on Confidentiality: good practice in handling patient information."

### Changes from the 2013 edition

- Para 69 replaces 2013 para 19, adding "contemporaneous" with definition.
- Para 70 replaces 2013 para 21, expanding from 5 to 7 sub-paragraphs — adds patient preferences/concerns (d), reasonable adjustments and communication support preferences (e), and a proportionality principle.
- Para 71 replaces 2013 para 20, adding explicit reference to GMC Confidentiality guidance.

### GMC Confidentiality: good practice in handling patient information (2017, updated 2024)

- **URL:** https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/the-professional-standards/confidentiality
- **Type:** GUIDANCE (professional standards)
- **Updated:** 25 May 2018 (GDPR/DPA 2018); 13 December 2024 (PA/AA regulation by GMC)

Sets out **eight principles of confidentiality** and a decision-making framework:
- Paras 1–7: Ethical and legal duties of confidentiality
- Paras 9–25: Disclosing patients' personal information: a framework (consent, public interest, data protection law)
- Paras 26–49: Using and disclosing patient information for direct care
- Paras 50–76: Disclosures for protection of patients and others
- Paras 77–116: Using and disclosing patient information for secondary purposes
- Paras 117–138: Managing and protecting personal information (records management, retention, access rights)

Para 1: "Trust is an essential part of the relationship between patients and medical professionals and confidentiality is central to this."

## 2. Common Law Duty of Confidentiality

- **Type:** LAW (common law — judge-made, not statute)
- **URL (DHSC summary):** https://www.health-ni.gov.uk/articles/common-law-duty-confidentiality

> "The general position is that if information is given in circumstances where it is expected that a duty of confidence applies, that information cannot normally be disclosed without the information provider's consent."

**Three circumstances making disclosure lawful:**
1. The individual has consented
2. Disclosure is necessary to safeguard the individual, others, or is in the public interest
3. There is a legal duty (e.g. a court order)

The duty of confidentiality **extends beyond death** (Bluck v IC and Epsom & St Helier NHS Trust, EA/2006/0010; Lewis v Secretary of State for Health [2008] EWHC 2196).

## 3. Data Protection Act 2018 / UK GDPR

- **DPA 2018:** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/contents — LAW (statute), in force 25 May 2018
- **UK GDPR:** retained EU GDPR as amended — LAW (statute)
- **ICO guidance:** https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/special-category-data

### Health data is special category data (Article 9)

Processing requires **both**:
1. A lawful basis under **Article 6** (e.g. (c) legal obligation, (e) public task)
2. A separate condition under **Article 9(2)** for special category data

Article 4(15) — health data: "personal data related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, which reveal information about his or her health status."

### Article 9(2) conditions

| Letter | Condition |
|--------|-----------|
| (a) | Explicit consent |
| (b) | Employment, social security and social protection (if authorised by law) |
| (c) | Vital interests |
| (d) | Not-for-profit bodies |
| (e) | Made public by the data subject |
| (f) | Legal claims or judicial acts |
| (g) | Reasons of substantial public interest (with a basis in law) |
| (h) | Health or social care (with a basis in law) |
| (i) | Public health (with a basis in law) |
| (j) | Archiving, research and statistics (with a basis in law) |

Five conditions (b, g, h, i, j) require additional conditions in **Schedule 1 of the DPA 2018**, and often an "appropriate policy document."

### NHS direct-care lawful basis

For direct care, NHS typically relies on:
- Article 6(1)(c) (legal obligation) or 6(1)(e) (public task)
- Article 9(2)(h) (health or social care) + Schedule 1, Part 1, paragraph 2 of the DPA 2018

## 4. The Eight Caldicott Principles (December 2020)

- **URL:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-caldicott-principles
- **Type:** GUIDANCE (National Data Guardian)

**Principle 1:** Justify the purpose(s) for using confidential information.
**Principle 2:** Use confidential information only when it is necessary.
**Principle 3:** Use the minimum necessary confidential information.
**Principle 4:** Access to confidential information should be on a strict need-to-know basis.
**Principle 5:** Everyone with access to confidential information should be aware of their responsibilities.
**Principle 6:** Comply with the law.
**Principle 7:** The duty to share information for individual care is as important as the duty to protect patient confidentiality.
**Principle 8:** Inform patients and service users about how their confidential information is used.

Every NHS organisation must have a **Caldicott Guardian**.

## 5. Access to Health Records Act 1990 & Access to Medical Reports Act 1988

### Access to Health Records Act 1990

- **URL:** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1990/23/contents — LAW (statute)
- Most living-patient access provisions were repealed on 1 March 2000 (now via UK GDPR subject access requests). **Surviving provisions apply primarily to deceased patients' records.**
- Access available to the **personal representative** of the deceased, or any person who **may have a claim arising out of the patient's death**.
- Grounds for refusal: likely serious harm to health of any individual; third-party information without consent; patient requested before death that access not be given; records before 1 November 1991.

### Access to Medical Reports Act 1988

- **URL:** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/28/contents — LAW (statute), in force 1 January 1989
- Gives patients rights to see medical reports written for **employment or insurance** purposes.
- Patient rights: withhold permission; access the report before it is sent or up to **six months** after; instruct the doctor not to send it; request amendment of inaccuracies.
- GMC (Confidentiality para 115): all doctors must offer individuals the opportunity to see a report written about them before it is sent.

## 6. NHS Records Management: NHS Code of Practice (2021, updated 2023)

- **URL:** https://transform.england.nhs.uk/information-governance/guidance/records-management-code/records-management-code-of-practice
- **Type:** GUIDANCE (NHS code of practice, underpinned by statutory obligations)

### Key retention periods (Appendix II)

| Record type | Retention period | Trigger |
|-------------|------------------|--------|
| Adult clinical records (NHS) | 8 years | After completion of treatment / death |
| Children and young people (under 18) | Until 25th birthday (or 26th if entry made at 17) | After completion of treatment |
| Maternity records | 25 years | End of calendar year |
| Mental health records | 20 years | Last contact |
| GP records (deceased patients) | 10 years | Date of death |

## 7. PRSB — Standards for the Structure and Content of Health and Care Records (2018)

- **URL:** https://theprsb.org/standards/
- **PDF:** https://www.rcp.ac.uk/media/3krn3jxl/prsb-structure-and-content-of-health-care-records.pdf
- **Type:** GUIDANCE (professional standard, commissioned by NHS Digital, developed with RCP Health Informatics Unit)

Adopted and extended the 2013 Academy of Medical Royal Colleges (AoMRC) standards. Defines structured content across use cases:

1. All record structured content
2. Admission record
3. Handover record
4. Discharge record
5. Outpatient letter
6. Crisis care plan
7. Clinical referral information
8. Healthy child record

Conformance is MRO: **M**andatory (MUST), **R**equired (SHOULD), **O**ptional (MAY). Value sets are SNOMED CT codes. Messages sent using HL7 FHIR.

Key headings: problems and issues, diagnoses (date of onset, severity, certainty), procedures (anatomical site, laterality, complications), medications, allergies and adverse reactions, relevant clinical risk factors, patient preferences, decisions made and actions agreed, responsible clinician (role, grade, specialty, contact details).

## 8. Coroners and Justice Act 2009

- **URL:** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2009/25/contents — LAW (statute), in force 25 July 2013

Section 1: a senior coroner must investigate if there is reason to suspect the deceased died a violent or unnatural death, the cause is unknown, or died in custody/state detention.

**Schedule 5, Paragraph 7 — Reports to Prevent Future Deaths (PFD reports):** a coroner must report a matter to a person who may have the power to take action if the investigation gives rise to a concern that circumstances creating a risk of other deaths will occur or continue. The recipient must respond within **56 days**.

Clinical records are key evidence in coroner investigations. Withholding or altering records in relation to a coroner investigation is an offence under Schedule 6 of the CJA 2009.

## 9. CQC — Regulation 17 (Good Governance)

- **URL:** https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/providers/regulations-service-providers-and-managers/health-social-care-act/regulation-17
- **Type:** LAW (regulation — Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014)

**Regulation 17(2)(c):**
> "...maintain securely an accurate, complete and contemporaneous record in respect of each service user, including a record of the care and treatment provided to the service user and of decisions taken in relation to the care and treatment provided"

CQC guidance: records must be "complete, legible, indelible, accurate and up to date, with no undue delays." Poor record-keeping is a common cause of CQC regulatory action — warning notices, requirements for improvement, prosecution, or cancellation of registration.

## 10. Duty of Candour — Regulation 20

- **CQC guidance:** https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/providers/regulations-service-providers-and-managers/health-social-care-act/regulation-20
- **Legislation:** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2014/9780111117613/regulation/20
- **Type:** LAW (regulation — HSCA 2008 Regulated Activities Regulations 2014)
- **In force:** NHS trusts from 27 November 2014; all CQC-registered providers from 1 April 2015

**Regulation 20(3) — notification must:**
> (a) be given in person by one or more representatives of the health service body,
> (b) provide an account of the incident, which to the best of the health service body's knowledge is true of all facts known at the time of the notification,
> (c) advise the relevant person what further enquiries the health service body believes are appropriate,
> (d) offer an apology, and
> (e) keep a written record of the notification and of all conversations and correspondence with the relevant person in relation to the incident.

Regulation 20(2)(a) and (3) are **criminal offences** if breached — CQC can prosecute directly.

"**Notifiable safety incident**" (Reg 20(8)): any unintended or unexpected incident that could result in, or appears to have resulted in, death, or severe/moderate/prolonged psychological harm.

"Saying sorry is not an admission of liability."

**Professional duty of candour** (GMC/NMC joint guidance, https://www.gmc-uk.org/ethical-guidance/ethical-guidance-for-doctors/openness-and-honesty-when-things-go-wrong--the-professional-duty-of-candour) applies in **all** circumstances, not just notifiable safety incidents.

## 11. Freedom of Information Act 2000

- **URL:** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/36/contents — LAW (statute), in force 1 January 2005

Provides public access to information held by public authorities (including NHS bodies). **Section 40** exempts personal data (accessible via UK GDPR subject access requests instead). **Section 41** exempts information provided in confidence. NHS bodies must follow the **Section 46 Code of Practice** on records management.

## Recent Changes (2023–2025)

1. **GMC Good Medical Practice 2024** (in effect 30 January 2024): record-keeping section expanded — paras 69–71 add patient preferences/concerns, reasonable adjustments, communication support, and a proportionality principle; "contemporaneous" added with definition.
2. **GMC Confidentiality guidance** updated 13 December 2024: extended to cover physician associates (PAs) and anaesthesia associates (AAs) now regulated by the GMC.
3. **NHS Records Management Code of Practice** updated 5 December 2023: new sections on integrated care, individual rights, personal health records, and social care.
4. **Duty of candour review** announced December 2023 (DHSC).
5. **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025**: codified ICO guidance on SAR responses ("reasonable and proportionate" search) and "stop the clock" rule for SAR deadlines. Core Article 9 framework for health data unchanged.
